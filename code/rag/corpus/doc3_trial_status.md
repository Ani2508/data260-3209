# Clinical Trial Status Definitions

Every clinical trial listed in a public registry is assigned a status that
describes where it currently stands in its lifecycle. Understanding these
statuses helps patients, researchers, and sponsors know whether a trial is
still an option to join or already finished.

## Recruiting

A trial marked "Recruiting" is actively looking for and enrolling new
participants who meet its eligibility criteria. This is the stage where
interested patients should contact the study team if they want to take part.

## Active, Not Recruiting

A trial marked "Active, Not Recruiting" is currently running -- participants
already enrolled are still being followed, treated, or monitored -- but the
study is no longer accepting new participants. This often happens once a
trial has reached its target enrollment number.

## Completed

A trial marked "Completed" has finished all planned activities, including the
follow-up period for its last enrolled participant. Results may or may not be
publicly posted yet; posting results can take additional time after a trial
reaches "Completed" status, since data must be analyzed first.

## Terminated

A trial marked "Terminated" was stopped early, before it reached its planned
completion. Common reasons for early termination include safety concerns
identified during the trial, insufficient enrollment, funding problems, or an
independent safety board recommending the trial stop because early results
already show a clear outcome, positive or negative.

## Other statuses

Some registries also use "Not yet recruiting" for trials that have been
registered but have not started enrolling anyone yet, and "Suspended" for
trials that have temporarily paused enrollment or activity but may resume
later, unlike a full termination.

## Why status matters for a listing

The status of a trial is one of the most important fields in its public
record, since it directly answers the practical question a patient or
researcher usually has first: is this trial something I can still join right
now, or not?
